About BurnoutLens & Methodology

Scientific background, model derivation, data lineage, and analytical limitations.

1. Data Lineage & Preprocessing

BurnoutLens is trained on the Kaggle Sleep Health and Lifestyle dataset consisting of 374 total rows. Crucially, data auditing revealed that the dataset contains only 132 unique behavioral profiles; the remaining 242 rows are exact duplicates (excluding synthetic Person ID).

To prevent statistical data leakage, preprocessing uses a scikit-learn ColumnTransformer fit strictly within cross-validation folds. Nominal features are one-hot encoded, continuous features are standard-scaled, and raw string artifacts (like combined Blood Pressure) are replaced by separate systolic and diastolic integers.

2. Target Definition & Ground Truth

The target variable Burnout Risk is derived deterministically from self-reported Stress Level (Low: 1–4, Medium: 5–6, High: 7–10). Consequently, the classifier predicts self-reported occupational stress rather than a clinically evaluated psychiatric outcome.

3. Explainability Architecture

Feature attributions use linear SHAP formulations on the model's decision_function log-odds scale: 

φj = wj · (xj − E[Xj])

The background baseline E[Xj] is computed over the 132 unique profiles to eliminate repeated duplicate distortion. One-hot indicator contributions are linearly summed to their original 12 source variables, preserving exact mathematical additivity to within 10-14.

4. Analytical Limitations & Dataset Artifacts

  • Observational Nurse Artifact: In this dataset, respondents with ≥ 10,000 steps and ≥ 90 minutes of activity are 94% nurses with 100% High burnout risk. Consequently, the model assigns positive risk weights to higher steps. This is a descriptive artifact of occupational sampling in this dataset, not health advice.
  • Context vs. Actionable Variables: Demographic variables (Gender, Age, Occupation) are non-actionable baseline modifiers.
  • Small Dataset: Trained on 374 survey rows. Probabilities represent relative model log-odds and are uncalibrated for true clinical epidemiology.
